# 06 — Product identity

**Recommendation: retain “task-native work instrument”; test “return to engineering judgment without reconstructing the task” as its promise.** Attention is the standard of quality, not a defensible category claim by itself. The current record justifies keeping a coherent personal instrument; it does not yet justify expanding or commercializing it.

## Competing identities

| Identity | Why someone might return | Fit with the instrument | Strongest objection | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task-native work instrument | One intent persists through execution/correction | Best fit with actual architecture and restrained identity | Describes internal ontology rather than a felt benefit | Better accurate return/correction than native resume at lower total effort |
| Personal delegation instrument | Give bounded work and resume responsibility later | Direct, humane without personification | Suggests reliable unattended work/alerts beyond current evidence | Ordinary tasks completed with low intervention and no additional missed defects, including setup/review |
| Lightweight engineering work inbox | A place for everything needing a decision | Could preserve minimal priority view | Duplicates issues, PRs and native inbox; invites project-management scope | Voluntary consolidation and repeated use without duplicate maintenance |
| Human attention layer | Required judgments arrive with context | Useful evaluation criterion | Vendors already ship notifications/triage; silence may conceal failure | Fewer unnecessary checks and fewer missed consequential stops |
| Independent continuity/accountability layer | Provider-independent inspectability | Local task ownership is real | Accountability is overstated without immutable attribution/acceptance evidence | Better constraint/evidence recovery and trustworthy scope after provider churn |
| Native-harness template/extension | Same benefit with fewer moving parts | Radical subtraction preserves purpose | Weakens separate-product ambition | Equal results/effort would favor this option |

Keep the hex identity, primitive visual language and direct verbs. Human-centered design does not require friendliness or an assistant persona. It requires that a result, action and consequence mean what they say. Industrial restraint is compatible with readable explanations; missing a conclusion is not a principled form of minimalism.

## Six-to-twelve-month scenario

Assume more capable agents, easier background execution and continuation, and accessible orchestration. This is a requested stress scenario, not a forecast.

**Likely absorbed or already commoditized:** starting a worker, reconnecting a session, switching providers, viewing tool events, status notifications, mobile approvals and basic multi-agent dispatch. These remain necessary foundations when used, but are weak reasons to install a separate product.

**Potentially durable human value:** remembering why work exists; knowing which constraints were actually delivered; finding relevant evidence and uncertainty; understanding consequences before authorizing; correcting intent without starting over; distinguishing useful work from a cleanly finished run. These needs can also be served by better harnesses. The durability is in the need, not exclusive ownership by 0x2F.

**Value that may shrink:** review effort, reconstruction and even ambiguity may fall if stronger agents produce excellent checked artifacts and native summaries. Do not assume agent progress inevitably creates more attention debt. More generated work can also increase integration/review load. Both mechanisms should be observed at actual task boundaries.

## Foundation versus user value

Task persistence, shared actions, atomic local records and run artifacts should stay foundations. The user should not have to learn a node taxonomy, maintain history manually or choose a provider on each ordinary task. The provider registry and event vocabulary serve execution; they do not need to dominate the interface. Four surfaces and unlimited history carry maintenance/context costs. Keep useful access, but postpone expansion until repeated use identifies which surface earns its cost.

The strongest surviving combination would be **small local ownership + faithful evidence + inexpensive reentry across attempts**. It is experiential and technical. It is not a guaranteed moat; another instrument could reproduce it. Distinction should come from the accumulated reliability of the loop and fit with real work, not a longer feature list.

## Separate-product necessity test

Compare current 0x2F, a narrow nonshipping fidelity/return prototype, a current native harness, and that harness with a simple task/result template. Use equal models, tools, repository snapshots and acceptance criteria. Include initialization, review and correction. After two sessions separated by a genuine delay, let people choose the tool for another task without prompting. Separate founder proficiency from unfamiliar developers.

Continue toward a product only if the extra layer repeatedly pays for itself in accurate understanding, recovered constraints and lower total active work. Recast it as a personal tool, extension or export format if native-plus-template performs equally well. Stop expanding if users admire its aesthetic but do their real acceptance elsewhere and do not return voluntarily.

## Scope discipline

Do not build an IDE, synthetic personality, fleet command center, project-management layer, semantic provider optimizer, mandatory verifier agent, confidence score or automatic cross-provider retry to defend the positioning. No evidence here establishes demand for any of them. Isolation may become a necessary correctness prerequisite if repeated concurrent use is demonstrated; that would justify a bounded policy, not a multiplexer ambition. Hosted infrastructure, push and team accounts require separate evidence and are outside the next mission.
