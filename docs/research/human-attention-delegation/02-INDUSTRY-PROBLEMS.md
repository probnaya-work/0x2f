# 02 — Industry problems

**Research cutoff: 2026-11-01.** The evidence supports a real distinction between coordinating execution and evaluating useful work. It does not establish the size of a market for an additional attention application. Studies, vendor claims and isolated issue reports answer different questions.

R1 is unusually relevant to simultaneous coding agents, but its completion measure omits much of the acceptance work this investigation concerns. R6 challenges equating an approval button with independent human judgment. Neither proves a specific 0x2F solution. R5 is a reason to record active human work separately from execution elapsed time, rather than a reason to pick a favorable productivity statistic.

## Answers to the ten research questions

| Question | Evidence-based answer | Interpretation for 0x2F / remaining unknown |
|---|---|---|
| 1. What forces supervision? | Scope/constraint setting, ambiguous intent, authorization, environment failure, outcome checking and cross-task coordination recur in R1/R2 and reports R8–R11 | Distinguish required judgment from avoidable checking. We do not know their relative frequency for 0x2F users |
| 2. Where is confidence lost? | Evidence can be hard to inspect and apparent output success can dominate review (R2/R4); the local live utility needed a correction after passing tests | A test count or provider conclusion alone is insufficient. Actual confidence of users here is unmeasured |
| 3. Necessary versus avoidable interruptions? | Broad preapproved operations can generate repeated requests (R7/R8); missing permission alerts can strand work (R9/R10) | Fewer prompts and reliable meaningful prompts are separate objectives. No universal permission-approval policy follows |
| 4. Returning hours later? | R1 externalizes session memory and triage; R2 emphasizes keeping implementation under expert control | Reorientation should retrieve intent, decisions, changes and missing checks. No multi-hour 0x2F human return was measured |
| 5. Why return to direct coding? | R2 describes deliberate expert involvement; R4 suggests difficulty staying engaged with agent process | Poor control, a mismatch or excessive verification cost are plausible mechanisms. We did not measure abandonment or causal reasons |
| 6. When do multiple agents add burden? | R1 separates decomposition/isolation/logging/monitoring; R11 reports concurrency complicating approvals | Shared checkout attribution and more simultaneous results can transfer work to review. Neither max agent count nor throughput is a human benefit by itself |
| 7. What makes results evaluable? | Concrete artifacts and checks matter; R2/R3 support studying verification behavior | Test a concise route from intent to evidence, with acknowledged gaps and full sources. A generated confidence score is unsubstantiated |
| 8. How much context reconstruction? | R1 establishes a mechanism and measures tracking in its apparatus | No general minute/hour estimate is defensible. Our command/character counts cannot supply one |
| 9. What becomes standard? | Current product docs already include resume, background work, review, remote approval and handoff; [03](03-COMPETITIVE-POSITION.md) records them | More availability is a scenario; the overlap is current fact at the vendor-promise level. Avoid monetizing a transient missing button |
| 10. What human needs remain? | Experts still frame purpose, weigh ambiguous alternatives and judge consequences (R2/R6) | Accountability and evidence retrieval plausibly endure, but better agents may reduce review cost themselves. Durable responsibility does not prove demand for an additional product |

## Workflow implications

For a **single agent**, dispatch can already be cheap; an extra ledger must earn its setup and review cost. For **parallel agents**, planning, separation and result integration matter as much as monitoring. For **background/cloud execution**, discoverability of stops and the location of the resulting work matter. For **long-running work**, replayable records can also accumulate stale or excessive context. For **cross-provider work**, capability differences and evidence fidelity matter more than preserving every transcript byte. For **agent review**, an additional agent is another source of claims unless its inputs, checks and independence can be examined.

The most credible unmet need to investigate is **avoiding reconstruction while retaining informed judgment**. The evidence rejects a stronger statement that developers universally want less involvement, or that the industry has no solution. Public issue reports are selected for explanatory value and are not a survey. No interviews, satisfaction measures, adoption statistics or willingness-to-pay evidence were manufactured.
