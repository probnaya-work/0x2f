# 10 — Executive verdict

**Keep 0x2F small and task-native. Make returning to a result and assuming responsibility easier before adding autonomy.** The investigation supports concrete fidelity work and a controlled product test. It does not validate “the missing human attention layer” as a market position.

1. **What genuinely makes it different today?** A small, inspectable, repository-local Task → Run → Session record that preserves original intent, human constraints and prior outcomes through different execution harnesses. The two retained live chains demonstrate real continuity. That combination is useful architecture; generic persistence and handoff are no longer unique capabilities.

2. **Which advantages are real versus aspirations?** Real: durable task context, recorded attempts, shared actions across surfaces, sequential admission, explicit permission/decision distinction and quiet Web progress policy. Aspirational: lower net human effort, reliable attention summons when away, independently trustworthy completion, universal cross-harness portability and repeated developer preference. None was established by test counts.

3. **Greatest source of human friction?** Recovering the meaning and assurance of the result from an execution-oriented record. The clearest example is the TUI: ACCEPT is available but the full brief and written result/Verification are not. Mobile can hide relevant caveats behind disclosure and overstate when a saved constraint applies. Source/fixture evidence proves availability and semantic gaps, not the size of their human cost.

4. **Primary product promise to test?** **“Delegate engineering work. Return to a clear account of what happened, what remains uncertain, and what needs your judgment.”** This is proposed test positioning, not approved marketing. “Trustworthy result” is too broad for the evidence currently attached to READY.

5. **What should be removed or simplified?** Unsupported claims that harnesses forget everything; routine provider/session/node choices in ordinary work; false current-run influence copy; implications that executable detection means authentication; duplicated history content when safely removable. Keep full evidence accessible. Make task correction one intention using the combined paths that already exist.

6. **What should absolutely not be built now?** A broad IDE, project manager, fleet dashboard, anthropomorphic chat personality, gamification, AI provider optimizer, automatic cross-provider failover, universal confidence score or mandatory verifier agent. Do not revive managed execution to manufacture differentiation. New infrastructure, surfaces and orchestration have no demand evidence here.

7. **What remains valuable with stronger agents?** Faithful intent and constraint delivery, retrieval of consequential evidence, honest uncertainty, clear authorization boundaries and cheap correction. Better models may supply these upstream too. Enduring human accountability does not automatically imply an enduring separate product.

8. **Why would a developer choose it repeatedly?** Because the total loop—setup, delegation, leaving, returning, checking and correcting—costs less active effort while preserving at least equal judgment, and the record remains theirs. Repeated voluntary choice must demonstrate this. Visual admiration, more agents or a second successful demo are insufficient.

9. **Strongest reason it might fail?** A native harness plus existing issue/PR review or a small template already solves the ordinary job well enough. 0x2F then adds another record, another surface and adapter maintenance without enough saved reconstruction. Cross-harness use may be too occasional to pay that cost.

10. **Three highest-value changes to investigate next?**
    - Restore access to existing original intent and written result/check limitations in the TUI, keeping trace secondary and the five-state lifecycle intact.
    - Make human control truthful: correct saved-note timing, and audit whether mobile permission views contain sufficient consequential scope before authorization.
    - Test a compact return-to-task presentation against current 0x2F, current native workflows and native-plus-template using the same artifacts/models, actual delayed return, acceptance accuracy and voluntary next-task choice.

## Suggested next engineering mission — not executed

**“Faithful return and intervention, with existing data.”** Make the two confirmed presentation contracts truthful and existing evidence reachable; qualify mobile permission sufficiency using representative safe fixtures. Preserve lifecycle, provider behavior, original run artifacts and product aesthetic. Do not add steering, generated summaries, new statuses, telemetry infrastructure or an evaluator.

Acceptance should include: a no-change investigation can be evaluated entirely in TUI; an unrun-check warning is discoverable before acceptance; the user can correctly tell when a saved note takes effect; an incomplete permission cannot be mistaken for fully inspected scope. Treat the current mock-harness retry failures as a separately scoped execution-ownership issue, not permission to bypass overlap protection.

Then run the prepared founder/unfamiliar-developer protocol. If the direct harness/template is equally good at lower total cost, reduce 0x2F's ambition or keep it a personal instrument. This conclusion should change with evidence, not with a desire to protect the thesis.

## What was completed

Ten reports, independent initial investigations and skeptical review, eight real-CLI deterministic cases, real PTY captures, narrow-browser evidence, independent classification/context/diff probes, a prepared realistic task pack with validated acceptance controls, and a human protocol. Historical live evidence was preserved and rechecked, not rerun. Current-host regression: **713 passed / 2 failed** (retry paths). Isolated regression: **704 passed / 1 failed / 10 skipped** (offline package-cache miss), separately retained in [08](08-DELEGATION-BENCHMARK.md). No production implementation, push, deployment, release or outreach occurred.
